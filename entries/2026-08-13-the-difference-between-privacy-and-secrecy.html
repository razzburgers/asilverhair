The Difference Between Privacy and Secrecy

Privacy protects a life. Secrecy protects a contradiction.

Not everyone is entitled to every detail of you.

You may keep parts of your history private. You may need time before introducing someone to family, sharing finances, or explaining an old wound. Privacy can be thoughtful. It gives intimacy somewhere to grow.

Secrecy feels different.

Secrecy usually requires management. Stories must be kept straight. Phones turn face down. Simple questions create disproportionate irritation. One version of the relationship exists in one room and another version exists somewhere else.

The distinction is not whether information is withheld. The distinction is what the withholding protects.

Privacy protects dignity, timing, safety, or independence. Secrecy often protects behavior that would become harder to continue if it were known.

You do not need access to every locked drawer in another person's life. But if you are repeatedly asked to ignore the sound coming from behind one of them, pay attention.

— Vivienne
