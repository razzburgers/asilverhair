The Conversation You Keep Rehearsing

Rehearsal can feel like preparation when it is really fear asking for certainty.

You can spend an astonishing amount of time preparing for a conversation that has not happened.

You imagine their objection, then improve your answer. You predict the silence after your confession and decide how long you will let it sit. You build three versions of yourself: the calm one, the clever one, and the one who will appear completely unbothered if everything goes badly.

Some preparation is useful. But eventually rehearsal becomes an attempt to control another person before they have even entered the room.

The trouble is that real people refuse to follow scripts. They answer the question you did not expect. They misunderstand the sentence you thought was perfect. Sometimes they are kinder than you prepared for. Sometimes they confirm the thing you were hoping not to know.

Decide what is actually yours to carry into the conversation: what happened, how it affected you, what you need now, and what you will do if that need cannot be met.

Leave the rest of the dialogue unwritten.

You do not need the perfect conversation. You need enough honesty to survive an imperfect one.

— Vivienne
